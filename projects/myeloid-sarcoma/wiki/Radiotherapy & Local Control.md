# Radiotherapy & Local Control

> [!abstract] Snapshot
> MS is **radiosensitive**. RT gives excellent **local control & palliation** but is **not curative alone** — it is an **adjunct** to [[Management|systemic therapy]].

## 🎯 Indications

> [!check] When to use RT
> - **Bulky, symptomatic** or **compressive** disease.
> - **Residual** disease after chemotherapy, or **chemo-refractory** lesions.
> - **Isolated extramedullary relapse** (including post-allogeneic transplant).
> - **Palliation**; or **consolidation** to a site of disease.
> - In patients **unfit for intensive chemo**, RT (± hypomethylating agent, e.g. **decitabine + RT**) can give durable local control.

## 📐 Dose & evidence

| Parameter | Detail | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Radiosensitivity | High; durable local control achievable | Bakst 2011 |
| Suggested dose | **≥ ~20 Gy**, e.g. **24 Gy in 12 fractions** | Bakst 2011 |
| Chloroma RT series (n=38, incl. breast) | Median **20 Gy**; 5-yr **PFS 39% / OS 43%**, minimal toxicity | Bakst 2011 |
| Adding local RT to systemic chemo | May **prolong failure-free survival**; OS benefit **not clearly shown** | Jain 2016 |

> [!note] Breast-specific examples
> - **Salvage RT** after chemo + allo-HCT (Bourque 2025).
> - Chemo (EMAP-type) + **consolidation RT** → durable remission (Zhang 2024).
> - Elderly, chemo-unfit: **decitabine + RT** → long-lasting control of **bilateral** breast MS (Minoia 2019).

> [!warning] Framing
> RT addresses the **local site only**. Without [[Management|systemic AML therapy]], marrow progression continues. Use RT **with**, not **instead of**, systemic treatment (except where intensive chemo is not feasible).
