# Definition & WHO Classification

> [!abstract] In one line
> **Myeloid sarcoma (MS)** is a tumour mass of **myeloid blasts** arising at an anatomical site **other than the bone marrow**, with effacement of the tissue architecture. Its diagnosis **is** a diagnosis of **acute myeloid leukaemia (AML)**.

## 📖 Definition

Myeloid sarcoma — historically **granulocytic sarcoma** or **chloroma** (the green hue reflects high myeloperoxidase) — is an extramedullary proliferation of immature myeloid cells forming a mass lesion. Synonyms are discouraged in favour of "myeloid sarcoma" because **~30% of cases are MPO-negative**.

> [!important] The governing rule
> A diagnosis of myeloid sarcoma is **equivalent to a diagnosis of AML**, *irrespective of the bone-marrow blast percentage*. Every case must then be **subclassified** using the same genetic/differentiation framework applied to marrow-based AML.

## 🧬 WHO 5th edition (2022) placement

In the **WHO Classification of Haematolymphoid Tumours, 5th edition (2022)**, AML is organised into two broad families, and **myeloid sarcoma is recognised as a distinct *presentation*** of AML that is then assigned to the appropriate subtype:

> [!note] WHO 5th ed — AML families
> **A. AML with defining genetic abnormalities**, e.g.:
> - APL with *PML::RARA*
> - AML with *RUNX1::RUNX1T1* — i.e. **t(8;21)** *(classic MS association)*
> - AML with *CBFB::MYH11* — i.e. **inv(16)/t(16;16)** *(breast/visceral tropism)*
> - AML with *KMT2A* rearrangement *(monocytic, skin/breast tropism)*
> - AML with *DEK::NUP214*, *NUP98* rearrangement, *BCR::ABL1*
> - AML with mutated *NPM1*
> - AML with *CEBPA* mutation
> - AML, myelodysplasia-related
>
> **B. AML defined by differentiation** (when no defining genetic lesion), e.g. AML with minimal differentiation / without maturation / with maturation; **acute myelomonocytic, acute monocytic, acute erythroid, acute megakaryoblastic** leukaemia.

> [!check] Practical consequence for breast MS
> After confirming MS on the [[Immunohistochemistry & Immunophenotype|IHC panel]], you **must obtain [[Cytogenetics & Molecular|cytogenetics & molecular]]** so the case can be slotted into one of the WHO genetic subtypes — this drives [[Management|treatment]] and [[Prognosis & Outcomes|prognosis]].

## 🔀 ICC 2022 — parallel framework

> [!info] International Consensus Classification (ICC, 2022)
> The ICC similarly treats myeloid sarcoma as a tissue manifestation of AML to be classified by genetics. Note minor threshold/category differences between WHO-5 and ICC (e.g. handling of the 10–19% blast range and certain *TP53*/MDS-related categories). For a given patient, **state which system you are using** in the report.

## 🕳️ Clinical scenarios recognised by the classification

| Scenario | Marrow at diagnosis | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **De novo / isolated (primary) MS** | Normal / non-diagnostic | Still classified & treated as AML — see [[Clinical Presentation]] |
| **Concurrent with AML** | Overt AML | MS = extramedullary disease |
| **Relapse / extramedullary recurrence** | ± marrow disease | Includes post-allograft "sanctuary" relapse |
| **Progression of MDS / MPN / MDS-MPN / CML** | Chronic neoplasm ± blasts | MS marks **blast transformation** |

> [!quote] Why the label matters
> Calling the breast lesion "myeloid sarcoma" rather than "a breast tumour" reframes the entire pathway: the patient now needs **AML staging and AML therapy**, not breast-cancer surgery.
