# Clinical Presentation

> [!abstract] Snapshot
> A **painless, firm, often rapidly enlarging** breast mass — unilateral or bilateral — **clinically and radiologically indistinguishable** from carcinoma or lymphoma. Arises in **four clinical contexts** that determine staging and treatment.

> [!quote]
> The mass looks like breast cancer, scans like breast cancer, and feels like breast cancer — until the **immunohistochemistry** says otherwise.

## 🧭 The four clinical contexts

> [!example] 1 — De novo / isolated (primary) MS
> - Breast mass with **no detectable marrow disease** or prior haematological diagnosis.
> - Bone marrow normal or non-diagnostic at presentation.
> - **Strongly predicts overt AML** — typically within months (reported progression up to **~95% within 1–48 months**) when not treated systemically.

> [!example] 2 — Concurrent with AML
> - Breast deposit found **at the same time** as a marrow diagnosis of AML.
> - Represents extramedullary disease within systemic AML.
> - Often **monocytic** differentiation / specific cytogenetic subgroups.

> [!example] 3 — Relapse / extramedullary recurrence
> - May herald **systemic relapse** of previously treated AML.
> - Includes **isolated extramedullary relapse after allogeneic transplant** (a "sanctuary site").
> - Mandates **restaging** of marrow and CNS, not just the local lesion.

> [!example] 4 — Transformation of MPN / MDS / CML
> - A breast deposit may signal **blast transformation** of an underlying chronic myeloid neoplasm.
> - Equivalent to progression to acute leukaemia — treat as such.
> - Review prior MPN/MDS/CML history and driver-mutation status.

## 🚩 Breast-specific features & traps

> [!warning] Easy-to-miss presentations
> - **Bilateral** breast masses (documented at presentation and at AML relapse).
> - **De novo breast MS that is actually APL** (*PML::RARA*) — would need **ATRA-based** therapy → full molecular workup is mandatory (see [[Cytogenetics & Molecular]]).
> - **Collision tumour**: MS coexisting with **invasive ductal carcinoma** in the same lesion — a core biopsy may capture only the carcinoma (see [[Differential Diagnosis & Pitfalls]]).

> [!tip] History to take
> Prior AML/MDS/MPN/CML, prior chemotherapy or transplant, B-symptoms, cytopenias, and rate of growth. These place the patient into one of the four contexts above and shape the [[Diagnostic Approach|work-up]].
